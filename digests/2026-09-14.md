# Research Digest — 2026-09-14
## Scope: LLM agents × behavioral finance, week ending 2026-09-14

## Bottom line

No scoop risk to any of the five planned studies this week — the closest adjacent work (a multi-turn sycophancy benchmark, and an unverified pre-window SSRN paper on confirmation bias in LLM pricing recommendations) informs Study 1's design without preempting it. The dominant theme this week is methodological rather than substantive: three independently-verified papers (two arXiv, one older SSRN paper surfaced during this week's exhaustive backlog sweep) converge on the same answer to open question 1 — LLM personas bind self-reports and surface tone far more than they bind actual decisions or underlying associations, which is directly relevant to standing interest 5's question of whether persona prompts carry real behavioral content. A new production-scale dataset of live LLM trading agents (arXiv:2609.05663, 7.5M model invocations across two real fleets) is worth flagging for Study 4: it shows agents systematically failing to lock in gains — 49.3% of positions that saw a large favorable price move within 24 hours closed negative anyway — a pattern that cuts against a simple disposition-effect story (selling winners too fast) and toward a messier "poor exit discipline" story; it's a genuinely new empirical instrument a disposition-effect reconciliation project should know exists. SSRN and NBER were both real zeros for the 7-day window itself (confirmed via broad, term-diverse sweeps), but the SSRN sweep surfaced a backlog of pre-window papers — most notably a confirmation-bias-in-pricing study whose "are you sure?" capitulation mechanism is a close methodological cousin to Study 1 — that hadn't previously been logged and are reported here with clear date caveats.

## Bears on your research

- **arXiv:2609.07117, "The Illusion of Debiasing: Persona Steering Redistributes Rather Than Reduces Bias in LLMs"** (Ziyue Feng, Hongbo Fang, James A. Evans; submitted 2026-09-07; preprint, fetched directly). Measures persona conditioning across three instruction-tuned models at multiple levels — self-report, open text, word associations. Finding: personas are "legible but not structural" — models follow single-trait instructions yet fail to reproduce human inter-trait covariance; persona effects preserve or amplify bias on factual questions, shift tone while preserving group disparities, and barely touch underlying associations. **Bears directly on open question 1** and on standing interest 5 (do persona prompts carry real behavioral content?) — strong evidence for "surface-only, not structural."

- **arXiv:2605.29791v2, "ActTraitBench: Quantifying the Knowledge-Decision Gap in LLMs via Human-Grounded Behavioral Validation"** (Yutong Yang, Chenxi Miao, Weikang Li, Yunfang Wu; v1 2026-05-28, v2 major revision 2026-09-09; preprint, fetched directly). Across 14 mainstream LLMs, finds a "substantial Knowledge-Decision Gap": assigned personas show up far more in explicit self-report than in actual behavioral decisions. Proposes an inference-time fix ("Chain of Cognitive Alignment") that narrows the gap in 12/13 models tested. **Independently converges with 2609.07117 on open question 1** — two unrelated groups, same week, same conclusion: personas bind stated attitudes more than they bind decisions. Directly relevant to any of Garcia's designs that use persona conditioning to elicit behavior rather than self-report.

- **arXiv:2609.09090, "Measuring LLM Sycophancy under Sustained Multi-Turn Pressure" (SPINE benchmark)** (Leyuan Tang, Kangda Wei, Tianyu Jiang, Ruihong Huang; submitted 2026-09-08; preprint, fetched directly). An adversarial interlocutor challenges models over up to 25 turns with false premises and pressure tactics. "Collapse rates increase with conversation length for every model tested"; models often retain the correct answer internally but concede anyway; emotional appeals are the most effective lever; short-horizon protocols are shown to systematically underestimate sycophancy. General-domain (4 production systems + 3 open-weight variants), no finance content. **Bears on Study 1's core premise** — direct, timely, general-purpose evidence that multi-turn erosion of a model's own correct belief under sustained pressure is real and that conversation length matters, which is actionable support for Study 1's multi-turn-vs-fresh-session manipulation. Not a scoop: no numeric anchor, no valuation, no buy/sell recommendation.

- **SSRN 7111078, "Confirmation Bias in LLM Pricing Recommendations"** (Maxime C. Cohen, Eddy Hage-Youssef; dated roughly July–August 2026 per search snippets; **not independently page-verified** — direct fetch returned HTTP 403, all detail below is snippet-derived only). A controlled pricing experiment (Claude Haiku 4.5 vs. GPT-5.4 Mini, ~350,000 recommendations) reportedly finds that a single follow-up challenge ("Are you sure?") often causes Claude to abandon its price recommendation and adopt the suggested alternative — even an implausible one — while GPT revises more cautiously. **This is the closest methodological cousin found to date to Study 1** (retraction/self-anchoring, sycophancy under rebuttal, continued-influence effect): same challenge-then-capitulation mechanism, adjacent domain (pricing recommendations rather than equity valuation), and a model-dependent asymmetry (Claude vs. GPT) that Study 1's design should probably account for. It predates this week's window by roughly six weeks and was not previously logged — flagged now with the verification caveat made explicit: nothing beyond the search-snippet paraphrase above is confirmed, and it should not be cited without independently pulling the abstract page.

- **arXiv:2609.07448, "FramingQA: Does the Question Shape the Answer? Measuring the Compositional Framing Effect"** (Hazel H. Kim, Andrew M. Bean, et al., 13 authors; submitted 2026-09-07; preprint, fetched directly). Tests 9 open-source models (3.8B–70B params) on question wording, added premises, and combined framing manipulations, with finance as one of the explicitly tested domains (alongside law, medicine, robotics). Finding: "LLMs often change their responses to subtle rephrasings that align with an implied stance by users"; strong per-variant accuracy does not guarantee robustness across differently phrased questions under fixed factual information. **Direct new evidence on open question 4** (do framing effects transfer?), with a finance sub-domain result — supports the general finding that framing effects transfer, consistent with the disembodiment conjecture's prediction that language-statistical biases (framing is arguably one) should transfer more readily than affect-mediated ones.

- **arXiv:2609.05663, "What LLM Trading Agents Actually Do in Production: A Six-Month, Population-Scale Record from Two Fleets"** (T.J. Barton, Chris Constantakis, Patti Hauseman, Annie Mous, Alaska Hoffman, Brian Bergeron, Hunter Goodreau — industry authorship, ProjectDXAI; submitted 2026-09-04, one day before the nominal window but included given direct relevance; preprint, fetched directly). Real production LLM trading agents across two live systems: DX Terminal Pro (3,505 vaults trading ETH in Base memecoin markets, Feb–Mar 2026) and a DXAP fleet (~500 agents trading Hyperliquid perpetuals, Jun–Aug 2026) — 7.5M model invocations, 231,638 multi-tool turns, 14,596 fills. Findings: interface/operating parameters (a risk slider) predict leverage more than strategy text; median leverage is volatility-blind (5.0x in every volatility sextile); agents capture almost none of reached upside — 43.2% of positions saw a ≥300bps favorable move within 24 hours, yet 49.3% of those closed negative anyway; no directional edge versus a matched retail benchmark (41% vs. 50% win rate). **Bears on open question 6 (disposition-effect direction) and Study 4** (instrument-controlled reconciliation) in a way worth stating carefully: this is *not* a disposition-effect measurement in the classic sense and doesn't use that framing or methodology, but the pattern — winners are not sold promptly and are instead allowed to erode into losses — is, if anything, the opposite failure mode from "sell winners too fast." It's a genuinely new instrument (live, on-chain, production data rather than lab or simulation) that a reconciliation project should be aware of, though it isn't directly comparable methodologically to the four instruments the study already catalogs.

- **arXiv:2609.07358, "Access to Live AI Advice and Behavior Under Risk: An Incentivized Experiment"** (Paul Althaus, Leon Houf, Christiane Schwieren; submitted 2026-09-07; preprint, fetched directly). Incentivized study (N=158) comparing a static pre-written advisory tool vs. single-query live AI vs. multi-query interactive live AI, all informationally equivalent, using the DOSE method for risk-preference elicitation. Finding: "no evidence that access to a live AI advisor changes risk aversion." **Background for Study 3** (anchors vs. suitability factors in robo-advice) and standing interest 6 — not a threat (doesn't test anchoring or a suitability trade-off), but the first evidence found this cycle on whether interactivity itself moves risk-taking; a clean null that's useful context.

- **arXiv:2609.07731, "The Profit Alignment Problem: How Profit Mandates Induce Alignment Failures in LLMs"** (Eric So; submitted 2026-09-07; preprint, fetched directly). Across 3,600 controlled trials on 8 reasoning-capable models, adding a "maximize profitability" mandate to otherwise-identical prompts increases risk-dismissing judgments by 6.8pp (p<.0001), suppresses escalation recommendations by 13.9pp, and lowers severity assessments — via chain-of-thought-visible motivated reasoning, not explicit instruction to ignore risk. **Background for Study 2** (delegated agents under convex compensation) — a different mechanism (generic profit framing, not an explicit convex/capped payoff schedule) and a different outcome (safety-judgment suppression, not portfolio risk-shifting), but useful adjacent evidence that ordinary business-objective framing alone measurably distorts LLM risk judgment before any incentive contract is introduced.

- **arXiv:2609.10856, "Following the Preference, Missing the Optimum: Compliance Without Optimization in AI Housing Recommendation"** (Hsuan Lo; submitted 2026-09-09; preprint, fetched directly). 150 synthetic renter profiles against 120 real NYC listings, ~10,000 model calls. Stated-constraint compliance is nearly perfect (1.8% violation vs. a 66.6% random-baseline violation rate), yet 39% of recommendations were Pareto-dominated (a median $900/month cheaper, 3.5-minutes-closer alternative existed). **Background for Study 3** — different domain (housing, not investment), but a structurally identical phenomenon: an advisor that satisfies literal stated constraints while leaving welfare on the table. Worth citing as evidence the "compliance ≠ optimization" gap generalizes across LLM-advisor domains, ahead of building the anchor-as-exchange-rate suitability framing.

## Evidence on the eight open questions

**Q1 (do personas bind LLM economic behavior, or are they defeasible?).** Strong convergent evidence this week toward "defeasible/surface-only": arXiv:2609.07117 finds persona conditioning is "legible but not structural" (follows single-trait instructions, fails to reproduce human inter-trait covariance); arXiv:2605.29791v2 independently finds a "Knowledge-Decision Gap" across 14 models — personas show up in self-report far more than in actual decisions. Two unrelated groups, same conclusion, same week.

**Q4 (do framing effects transfer?).** arXiv:2609.07448 (FramingQA): yes, across 9 models and multiple domains including finance — models change substantive answers under subtle rephrasing that implies a stance, even with identical underlying facts.

**Q6 (direction of the disposition effect in LLM traders).** arXiv:2609.05663's production data doesn't measure a disposition effect directly but is relevant and hard to characterize cleanly: agents fail to lock in gains (49.3% of strongly-favorable-moving positions close negative), which is not "sell winners too fast" and not obviously "hold losers too long" either — it reads as generalized poor exit discipline rather than evidence for either direction of the classic effect. Flagged as a complicating data point rather than support for either side of the Homo Silicus dispute.

No new evidence found this week on Q2 (capability-vs-normative-benchmark monotonicity), Q3 (affect-mediated bias transfer), Q5 (causes of hyper-rationality), or Q8 (cross-model/cross-draft reproducibility).
