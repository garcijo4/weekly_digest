**1. Ross & Lo, "One Size Fits None: Heuristic Collapse in LLM Investment Advice"** (arXiv:2604.23837, submitted Sun 26 Apr 2026 — ~15 weeks old). Authors: Jillian Ross and Andrew W. Lo (MIT). Verbatim abstract (fetched directly): *"We investigate whether frontier LLMs actually [integrate a user's full context], or whether they instead exhibit heuristic collapse: a systematic reduction of complex, multi-factor decisions to a small number of dominant inputs. We study the phenomenon in investment advice... we find systematic heuristic collapse: investment allocation decisions are largely determined by self-reported risk tolerance, while other relevant factors contribute minimally. We further find that web search partially attenuates heuristic collapse but does not resolve it."*
   — **Threatens Study 3** (anchors vs. suitability factors in robo-advice) only partially. It establishes that LLM advisors collapse suitability determinations onto risk tolerance and neglect other client factors — same domain (individualized investment/robo-advice, legal-suitability framing) as Study 3. But it does not touch anchoring at all, and does not attempt the exchange-rate framing (anchor bias worth N years of investment horizon) that is Study 3's specific contribution. What remains unclaimed: the anchor-vs-suitability-factor tradeoff itself, expressed as a substitution rate, and any connection to Reg BI / FINRA 2111. Worth reading before finalizing Study 3's design, since a reviewer may ask how it relates.

**2. Itkin, "Delayed Verification Destabilizes Multi-Agent LLM Belief: Instability Thresholds and Optimal Corrector Placement"** (arXiv:2606.27409, submitted 25 Jun 2026 — ~7 weeks old). Single author: Igor Itkin. Verbatim abstract (fetched directly): *"We model this process as delayed consensus on a graph with grounded corrector nodes. Spectral decomposition by the grounded Laplacian yields a closed-form stability threshold for the verification dose: correction that is too strong or too delayed can turn consensus into oscillation... The same framework gives a supermodular placement objective and a greedy (1-1/e)-approximation rule for assigning a limited corrector budget to influential nodes. Experiments across five open models confirm the predicted dose-delay oscillations. By contrast, grounded factual answering makes truth an absorbing boundary and eliminates the effect, suggesting that the instability is specific to signed-belief tasks."*
   — **Adjacent to Study 5** (anchor half-life in an agentic pipeline, verification-hop treatment). It builds formal machinery for exactly the class of question Study 5 asks — how much verification, placed where, stabilizes vs. destabilizes belief propagating through an LLM agent chain — and finds a real result (verification can *worsen* things if mistimed) that Study 5's design should account for. But the task is abstract "signed-belief" propagation on synthetic graphs, not financial valuations, and there is no transmission-coefficient / half-life framing and no dollar-anchor. What remains unclaimed: the entire financial-valuation instantiation, per-hop transmission coefficients for a contaminated numeric anchor specifically, and the empirical comparison of verification-hop treatments in that setting. Still, Study 5 should cite and differentiate from this, since the corrector-placement result is a real prior finding in adjacent territory.
