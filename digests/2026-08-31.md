## Scoop risk

**Study 1 (retraction and self-anchoring in valuation).** arXiv:2608.25869, "Anchoring Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge Systems: Prior Scores Compromise Evaluation Independence" (Kapetanovic, Altwlkany, Mercep, Duricic, Lacic; submitted Aug 26, 2026, cs.CL, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase from the fetched arXiv page): across 192,000 evaluation attempts and three conditions (no metadata, revision framing, anchored metadata carrying revision/attempt/prior-score fields), 7 of 8 LLM judges show scores shifting toward injected prior scores (Cohen's d up to 0.71); token-level analysis suggests a threshold response — anchor *presence* matters more than anchor *magnitude* within range; on categorical data with ground truth, anchored metadata blocks 48% of error corrections and flips 10% of correct judgments to wrong labels; neither chain-of-thought reasoning nor an explicit "disregard this metadata" warning eliminates the effect.
— This is the nearest thing found to Study 1's mechanism: does a model's own (or an injected) prior estimate anchor and resist correction. It does not scoop the design — it's LLM-as-judge scoring, not equity valuation; the "prior" is injected structured metadata, not the model's own laundered output surviving in conversational context; there's no multi-turn-vs-fresh-session contrast; and there's no buy/sell-recommendation-updates-with-valuation test. What remains fully unclaimed: the specific question of whether the un-retractable carrier is the anchor itself or the model's own copy of it (this paper only tests injected anchors, never the model's-own-prior-output condition), the financial-valuation domain, and the recommendation-updating causal chain. But the finding that neither CoT nor an explicit debiasing instruction works generalizes uncomfortably close to what Study 1 will need to show doesn't happen (or does) for a genuine multi-turn self-anchor — worth reading closely and citing as related work, and worth not letting this study season further before drafting.

No new work this week threatens Studies 2 (convex-compensation risk-shifting), 3 (anchor-as-suitability-exchange-rate), 4 (instrument-controlled disposition-effect reconciliation), or 5 (agentic-pipeline anchor half-life) — confirmed by targeted searches within each source-specific sweep (see Searches run). One SSRN candidate is close enough in spirit to Study 4 to flag for a later check: Cataneo, "Provider-Dependent Behavioral Asymmetries in LLM Trading Agents" (SSRN abstract_id=6776298), which varies *provider* rather than *instrument* at fixed model/price-path, per a search-snippet abstract (not independently verified — SSRN itself could not be fetched this week; posting date reported as May 2026, outside window, but a revision could have landed inside it).

## Bears on your research

- **arXiv:2608.22852, "Your AI, On a Dial: Controlling Investment Bias in LLMs with a Single Neuron"** (Park, Park, Lee, Kwon, Ahn, Choi, Lopez-Lira, Kim, Choi, Kong, Lee; submitted Aug 24, 2026; cs.AI/cs.CL/q-fin.GN, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase): an inference-time intervention on a single neuron produces a continuous, monotonic "investment-bias dial" shifting a model's aggregate buy/sell stance across five open-weight models, without touching prompts or weights; the shift changes the evidential emphasis of generated rationales, alters what an agentic retrieval pipeline searches for and selects, and propagates into security rankings and backtested portfolio composition. The dial's effect is stable as context length grows, while an equivalent system-prompt instruction progressively attenuates over long context. **Supports** Algorithmic Anchoring's core claim that a biasing signal, once introduced, propagates selectively into downstream buy/sell recommendations — here via a mechanistic rather than prompt-embedded channel, which strengthens the general claim that contamination-into-recommendations is not an artifact of prompt engineering specifically. It also **bears directly on open question 1** (persona bindingness): instruction-level steering (the system-prompt condition) is shown to be defeasible — it fades over long context — while a representation-level intervention does not, which is concrete evidence for "personas are defeasible instruction sets, but not all bias sources are personas." Lopez-Lira's co-authorship links this directly to the already-tracked "Can LLMs Trade?" line; recommend reading closely for citation in both working papers.

- **arXiv:2608.28182, "SILICA: Benchmarking large language model agent societies against human behavioural distributions"** (Raad Bin Tareaf; submitted Aug 28, 2026; physics.soc-ph/cs.CL, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase): a benchmarking instrument testing whether 12 open-weight models, run through five environments with published human behavioral anchors, (a) match human behavioral distributions, (b) survive apparatus perturbations that leave the underlying rules unchanged, and (c) show genuine social interaction versus reproduction of memorized experiments. Agreement with humans holds only for starting points (e.g., first-round public-goods contributions), not end states, and no model reproduces the human "corridor of cooperation." Merely reordering how two actions are listed costs one model 58 points of cooperation. In an ultimatum-style task, only one reasoning-trained model places its threshold where incentives require. Shared conventions form via a prior over names rather than genuine negotiation, but negotiation reappears once that prior is disrupted. **Complicates the "capability moves behavior toward the normative benchmark" story** (open question 2) — evidence here is that capability effects are heterogeneous and non-monotonic across tasks, corroborating Keshavarz, Seagraves & Sirmans (SSRN 5998954) in a different empirical setting. Also **bears on open question 1** (priors/conventions are disrupted rather than robustly bound) and on standing interest 5 (LLM-as-subject methodology) — the prompt-order sensitivity finding (58-point swing from reordering two listed actions) is a direct, quantified instance of the reproducibility/prompt-sensitivity concern that runs through all of Garcia's own designs, and is worth using as a robustness-check template.

- **arXiv:2608.28399, "RetailAgent: Structured Adverse Timing in Self-Conditioned Multimodal LLM Trading Agents"** (Zhang, Jiang, Huang, Li, Chen; submitted Aug 28, 2026; cs.AI/q-fin.TR, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase): LLM agents choosing long/flat on anonymized intraday equity paths show persistent *negative* timing (exposure-matched long/flat calls anti-correlated with subsequent returns), robust across modality, horizon, state, and model family; shuffling the agent's own saved action history attenuates the effect, and feeding the agent its own past decisions as memory increases policy persistence and *worsens* the adverse timing. **Bears on open question 6** (direction of systematic LLM trading behavior) with a new, specific finding — persistent adverse rather than favorable timing — and directly parallels the mechanism at the heart of both working papers: the model's own prior outputs, carried in context, contaminate later decisions and get worse with repetition. This is a same-session, single-agent analog of exactly the "own laundered output as an un-retractable carrier" question Study 1 is designed to isolate, and of the chain-transmission logic behind Study 5. Worth citing as evidence that self-conditioning on one's own outputs is a general LLM-trading-agent phenomenon, not specific to valuation anchoring.

- **arXiv:2608.23524, "The Measurement Revolution? Credible Measurement and Inference in the Age of AI"** (Dell & Rambachan; submitted Aug 24, 2026; econ.GN/cs.AI/stat.AP, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase): reviews how AI, including LLMs, is reshaping economic measurement by converting unstructured data into structured variables at scale, and argues credible inference requires validation anchored to explicit criteria rather than informal proxy justification; shows validation samples support valid inference even when AI predictions are arbitrarily biased, and discusses inference without a random validation sample. **Bears on standing interest 5** (statistical inference on model-generated data) — a high-profile methodological reference point for exactly the kind of "treat the LLM's output as the measured outcome" designs all five planned studies rely on. Not finance-specific, but worth citing for methodological defense.

- **arXiv:2608.26327, "How Unlikely Is 'Unlikely'? Assessing Verbal Probability Perception Across Large Language Models"** (Petridis, Pelechrinis, Obradovic; submitted Aug 26, 2026; cs.CL/cs.AI, preprint). Verified abstract (paraphrase): cross-model study of word-to-number mapping for 11 verbal-uncertainty expressions across 19 models, benchmarked against established human data with a bidirectional roundtrip-consistency test; models largely track human ordinal structure and reproduce "possible"'s documented human bimodality, but show a systematic *upward* bias for negative expressions ("unlikely," "improbable"); explaining answers reduces within-model variance but increases between-model divergence. **Bears on standing interest 1** (a documented human bias/heuristic partially reproducing, partially distorting, with a clean directional asymmetry) and **open question 8** (reproducibility across models — 19 tested, explicit divergence result). Also practically relevant to standing interest 6: an upward skew on negative-outcome verbal probabilities has direct implications for LLM-as-advisor risk disclosure and suitability communication.

- **PNAS, "Personality pairing improves human–AI collaboration"** (Ju & Aral; published Aug 28, 2026, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2530627123; peer-reviewed). Verified abstract (paraphrase from Crossref metadata): a preregistered experiment (1,258 participants, 7,266 ads, 1,168 raters) pairing humans with AI agents assigned Big Five personality traits for collaborative ad creation, plus a live field test on X (~5M impressions); personality-pairing effects on output quality were found (e.g., extraverted-human + conscientious-AI pairs produced the lowest-quality output), and field click-through/cost-per-click performance tracked the lab quality ratings. **Bears on open question 1** from the opposite direction of the arXiv findings above: here, an assigned AI persona has a durable, real-world-consequential causal effect on collaborative output, rather than fading or being overridden — a data point that personas can bind in some task settings even as they're shown to be defeasible in others (SILICA, the investment-bias dial's system-prompt condition). Not financial, but the mixed picture across these three papers this week is itself the interesting fact: persona bindingness looks task- and pressure-dependent rather than uniform in either direction, which is exactly the "no clean answer yet" state open question 1 is in.

## Evidence on the eight open questions

**Q1 (do personas bind LLM economic behavior, or are they defeasible?).** Mixed evidence this week, all discussed above: arXiv:2608.22852 shows instruction-level (system-prompt) steering fades over long context while a mechanistic intervention persists (defeasible); SILICA (arXiv:2608.28182) shows shared conventions/priors are disrupted rather than robustly held; PNAS (Ju & Aral) shows an assigned persona causally and durably shapes real-world collaborative output (binding). Net: bindingness looks contingent on task structure and pressure, not resolved either way.

**Q2 (does capability move behavior toward the normative benchmark, monotonically or not?).** SILICA's cross-task, cross-model results corroborate the non-monotonic picture from Keshavarz, Seagraves & Sirmans (SSRN 5998954): agreement with human behavior holds for starting points but not end states, and only one (reasoning-trained) model places its ultimatum threshold where incentives require.

**Q6 (direction of the disposition effect / systematic trading bias in LLM traders).** arXiv:2608.28399 (RetailAgent) reports a new, specific direction — persistent *adverse* timing (anti-correlated with subsequent returns), worsening when the agent is fed its own past decisions as memory.

**Q8 (reproducibility across model versions/drafts).** arXiv:2608.26327 tests 19 models and finds a systematic, model-general upward bias for negative verbal-probability expressions alongside model-specific divergence when reasoning is elicited; SILICA separately stress-tests reproducibility under trivial apparatus perturbations (the 58-point swing from reordering two listed actions) and finds it fragile.

No new evidence found this week on Q3 (affect-mediated bias transfer), Q4 (framing-effect transfer), Q5 (causes of hyper-rationality), or Q7 (bubbles/mispricing in LLM agent markets).

## Other new work in the intersection

- **arXiv:2608.22697, "Does Rank Still Matter? Position Bias When AI Agents Shop on Our Behalf"** (Wadi & Ma; submitted Aug 24, 2026, revised Aug 28, 2026; cs.AI/econ.GN, preprint). Randomizing the order of 100 hotel listings across 5,000 AI-agent sessions across four LLMs, compared against human field data: AI agents search far more exhaustively than humans and never decline to buy; listing position still predicts inspection but weakly and non-monotonically; all models converge on the same undominated listing regardless of position. Not finance, but a clean non-finance analog of attention/salience-driven choice divergence from humans (standing interest 3) and of the non-monotonic capability-vs-human-benchmark theme running through this week (open question 2).
- **JFE, "Intellectual property protection lost and competition: An examination using large language models"** (Acikalin, Caskurlu, Hoberg, Phillips; Vol. 182, Art. 104306, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104306; peer-reviewed, indexed just at/before the window). Uses an LLM purely as a text-classification tool to measure firms' patent-exposure from a Supreme Court ruling; finds large firms gain and small firms lose from weakened IP protection. LLM-as-measurement-instrument corporate finance, not LLM-as-behavioral-subject — no bearing on Garcia's program, noted for completeness only.
- **Finance Research Letters, "Can AI match professional analysts? Evidence from a multi-agent system"** (Francis & Zhang; Vol. 103, Art. 110127, DOI 10.1016/j.frl.2026.110127; peer-reviewed). A multi-agent LLM system generates equity research reports rated against Value Line by blinded human and LLM evaluators; general-population and LLM-judge raters preferred the AI reports. Touches the capability-vs-human-benchmark theme tangentially (output-quality perception, not bias divergence) and appears to have circulated since March 2025 with the August 2026 date reflecting Elsevier's issue assignment rather than new results — not reported as new research.
