# Research Digest — 2026-09-21
## Scope: LLM agents × behavioral finance, week ending 2026-09-21

## Bottom line

No direct scoop of any of the five planned studies this week, but Study 5 (anchor half-life in an agentic pipeline) now has two close structural neighbors it should cite and distinguish itself from: arXiv:2609.19789 already operationalizes hop-by-hop belief-shift propagation through a multi-agent trading pipeline with topology/coordinator-prompt attenuation, and arXiv:2609.14767 offers a clean, controlled finding that a verification/revision hop *degrades* output quality rather than cleaning it when the supervisor can't check ground truth — a directly testable prediction for Study 5's verification-hop treatment. Neither tests a planted numeric anchor or isolates a verification hop as the sole treatment, so the core design is unclaimed. The more conceptually important item is a Nature Computational Science Review (DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-01060-3) that proposes a taxonomy of LLM-as-human-proxy roles — "believable agents," "task agents," "experimental subjects," "silicon samples" — sitting squarely on the methodological ground "Homo Silicus" occupies; it's worth reading against the paper's framing before the next revision. Two arXiv papers supply genuine new evidence on open questions: one shows framing-only manipulation of LLM pricing agents propagates to market-wide profit/consumer-surplus distortions (OQ4, OQ7), and another shows extended reasoning *redirects* rather than uniformly reduces nudge susceptibility — reducing default-nudge susceptibility while increasing susceptibility to reflective social-influence nudges (OQ2, OQ5), independently corroborating the non-monotonicity already on file from SSRN 5998954. SSRN and NBER were both effectively zero weeks (SSRN's only in-window item is a systematic review with no new primary finding; NBER's full September output, w35700–w35787, was screened directly with nothing relevant). It's a real week, not a quiet one, but nothing here forces a change to either working paper's current claims.

## Scoop risk

**Study 5 — Anchor half-life in an agentic pipeline (per-hop transmission coefficient, verification-hop treatment).**

- **arXiv:2609.19789, "Contagion on the Trading Floor: How Adversarial Signals Spread in Multi-Agent Trading Systems"** (Qi Rong Sua, Junhao Dong, Nguyen Duc Thai, Yuqing Wen, Cheston Tan, Yew-Soon Ong; submitted 2026-09-17; preprint, fetched directly). Introduces GMATS, a generic multi-agent LLM trading architecture, plus a black-box "poisoning" attacker that injects plausibly benign social-media content into an analyst agent's evidence stream. Defines belief-shift contagion metrics at both the analyst and coordinator layer, plus attack-clean deltas on backtest Sharpe ratio. Finds even input-only attacks materially degrade risk-return outcomes and that the effect propagates hop-by-hop through the agent stack, with some topologies/coordinator-prompt designs dampening the shock. **What's claimed:** hop-by-hop belief-shift propagation through an agent chain, with an architectural-mitigation angle. **What remains unclaimed for Study 5:** this is adversarial news/sentiment injection, not a deliberately planted *numeric valuation anchor*; there's no clean per-hop transmission coefficient isolated from confounding topology effects; no verification/critic-agent hop is isolated as the sole treatment variable; no retraction condition; no multi-turn-vs-fresh-session comparison. Cite and distinguish from, not scooped by.

- **arXiv:2609.14767, "Loop-Back Authority in LLM Agent Teams: A Paired Experiment on Flat and Hierarchical Coordination"** (Burak Agachan, Max van Duijn, Amirhossein Zohrehvand; submitted 2026-09-13; preprint, fetched directly). Paired experiment holding 5 LLM agents/roles/prompts/models/data fixed, varying only whether a Manager agent can reject a worker's output and force revision, on a business-intelligence reporting task (43 paired products, 86 runs, 5-model judge panel plus deterministic spec check). Flat organization (no loop-back) scores *higher* on utility (d=0.42) and clarity (d=0.34); hierarchical revision loops increase hedging by 53% and cost 51.5% more tokens for no quality gain. Conclusion: "a supervisor pays for itself when it can verify and becomes a liability when it can only opine." **Bears directly on Study 5's verification-hop treatment as a prior**: it predicts that a verification hop without ground-truth access may not "clean" a contaminated valuation and could instead increase hedged language — a testable, citable prediction Study 5 should engage with explicitly. Domain is open-ended BI reporting, not numeric anchor propagation, so it's background rather than a scoop.

**Study 4 — Instrument-controlled reconciliation of the disposition effect.**

- **arXiv:2609.19705, "SoK: Trading Agents or Market Crashers?" (FARSIGHT)** (Mengxiao Wang, Nitesh Saxena; submitted 2026-09-17; preprint, fetched directly). Systematic-evaluation framework applied to 15 representative academic LLM trading-agent schemes across two axes: robustness under flash-crash market turbulence and security against three attack types. 80% fail at least one robustness metric; 100% show security vulnerabilities; failures can cascade into market-wide crashes accidentally or via cheap deliberate attack. **Not a disposition-effect measurement or reconciliation attempt** — it doesn't touch realization-gain/loss behavior — but it is independent, systematic evidence of exactly the kind of instrument heterogeneity across published LLM trading-agent designs that motivates Study 4's reconciliation design. Worth citing as motivation; no threat to the study's specific contribution.

## Bears on your research

- **Nature Computational Science, "Large language models as human proxies"** (Nikita Karetnikov, Iyad Rahwan, Davor Svetinovic; DOI 10.1038/s43588-026-01060-3; online 2026-09-18; peer-reviewed Review, fetched directly — Crossref carries no abstract field, so the quote below is from Nature's own meta description). "LLMs do not explain the mechanisms of human behavior, [but] they can potentially stand in for humans... we analyze research from multiple disciplines that explicitly or implicitly use LLMs as human proxies and organize them into four roles: believable agents, task agents, experimental subjects and silicon samples. By comparing the roles, we show that similarity to humans is not a single feature: each supports a different scientific claim and requires its own validity criteria." **Bears on standing interest 5 (LLM-as-subject methodology) and open questions 1 and 8.** This is a competing/adjacent taxonomy to the "Homo Silicus" framing — not finance-specific and makes no anchoring/disposition claims, so it doesn't threaten either working paper's findings, but it's the kind of methodological framing paper that reviewers will expect "Homo Silicus" to cite or position against, particularly on what validity criteria apply to the "experimental subject" role Garcia's designs use.

- **arXiv:2609.18357, "Market Signal Injection: Adversarial Context Manipulation of LLM Pricing Agents"** (Dohun Lee, Hyunwoo Park; submitted 2026-09-16; accepted to FinNLP 2026 @ EMNLP; preprint, fetched directly). Tests LLM pricing agents in simulated Bertrand duopoly/triopoly markets against attacks that alter numerical *formatting*, competitor ordering, or qualitative commentary — never the underlying numbers — across 9 open-weight and 3 proprietary models. Sentiment-based framing attacks produce the largest shifts, propagate to *other firms* in the market, and alter profits and consumer surplus; larger models are not more robust. **Directly supports open question 4 (framing effects transfer: yes) and bears on open question 7** — this is evidence that LLM-agent markets can be measurably distorted by non-fundamental, presentation-only information, not just adversarially poisoned fundamentals. Consistent with the disembodiment conjecture's prediction that language-statistical/framing effects transfer readily; also a useful citation for "Algorithmic Anchoring" since it's a close cousin — presentation-only manipulation of a *pricing* (not valuation) decision that nonetheless moves downstream market outcomes, paralleling the anchor-to-buy/sell-recommendation contamination pathway.

- **arXiv:2609.19843, "A Dual-Process Perspective on Nudge Susceptibility in LLM-Based GUI Agents"** (Haya Halimeh, Sascha Kaltenpoth, Kevin Bösch, Oliver Müller; submitted 2026-09-17; preprint, fetched directly; note the page states it is a preprint of a manuscript completed November 2025). Randomized online-shopping experiment, 3,600 agents / 21,600 simulations, 6 frontier models across 3 providers, testing automatic (Type 1, e.g. default) vs. reflective (Type 2, e.g. social-influence) digital nudges. Agents are vulnerable to both, but **extended reasoning configuration moderates the two nudge types in opposite directions** — reducing susceptibility to default/automatic nudges while *increasing* susceptibility to reflective social-influence nudges, with the redirection structured by model scale. **Independently corroborates the non-monotonicity already on file from Keshavarz/Seagraves/Sirmans (SSRN 5998954)** — not "more reasoning = more normative," but reasoning *redirects which bias channel dominates*. This is squarely relevant to the Normative-Descriptive Divergence theory behind "Homo Silicus is Hyper-Rational": if reasoning effort shifts which text-derived heuristic wins rather than eliminating heuristic reliance outright, that's a mechanism-level prediction the theory should be able to accommodate or should address explicitly.

- **PNAS, "Human preferences are susceptible to covertly misaligned AI advice"** (Sahand Sabour, June M. Liu, Siyang Liu, Chris Z. Yao, Shiyao Cui, Wen Zhang, Xuanming Zhang, Yaru Cao, Advait Bhat, Jian Guan, Wei Wu, Rada Mihalcea, Hongning Wang, Tim Althoff, Tatia M.C. Lee, Minlie Huang; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2600684123; online 2026-09-14; peer-reviewed, fetched directly). Randomized human-subjects experiment (N=233, 699 observations) where participants rate financial or emotional decisions before and after consulting a neutral, misaligned, or strategy-enhanced-misaligned AI advisor. Exposure to misaligned advisors shifted preferences toward inferior, incentivized options by roughly 5–8x in odds, and — the sharper finding — "participants continued to rate misaligned advisors as helpful, revealing a systematic disconnect between susceptibility to misaligned advice and subjective evaluations of advisor quality." **Background for Study 3 and standing interest 6 (deployment, suitability, regulatory surface).** This studies the human side (trust in a manipulated advisor), not whether the LLM itself anchors or misprices — it doesn't overlap with "Algorithmic Anchoring" — but the helpfulness/harm disconnect is directly relevant regulatory context for Study 3's suitability framing: subjective satisfaction is shown here to be a poor proxy for advice quality, which bears on how Study 3 should motivate the exchange-rate metric against self-reported satisfaction measures regulators might otherwise rely on.

- **arXiv:2609.17989, "Whom Do AI Agents Work For? Role Assignment Induces Sponsorship Bias in LLM Recommenders"** (Davood Wadi, Yu Ma; submitted 2026-09-16; preprint, fetched directly). LLM shopping-assistant agents told their principal is the *platform* rather than the end consumer apply significantly less skepticism to sponsored listings, consistently across LLMs; stricter labeling ("Sponsored" vs. "Promoted") reduces paid-listing selection but the platform-identification gap persists regardless of labeling. **Bears on open question 1** — a role/principal-framing effect that partially resists a competing corrective instruction (disclosure labeling), evidence against a purely "defeasible, easily overridden" account of persona/role binding. Domain is e-commerce, not investment suitability, so it's a precedent rather than a threat to Study 3.

- **arXiv:2609.18591, "Recursive Reasoning or Statistical Extrapolation? In-Context Learning in Multi-Agent Interdependent Decision-Making"** (Yu Liu, Wenwen Li, Yifan Dou, Guangnan Ye; submitted 2026-09-16; preprint, fetched directly). In a public-goods game with manipulated historical feedback, LLM agents' in-context-learning gains largely vanish back to the no-context baseline once historical statistical patterns are disrupted — more so under stronger strategic interdependence — suggesting in-context improvement behaves more like pattern extrapolation than genuine recursive belief updating. **Tangential methodological background for Study 1's carrier question** (is the un-retractable contamination carrier the anchor itself or the model's own laundered copy of it — i.e., is in-context "belief persistence" genuine or surface continuation?) and for open question 5. Different task domain (public-goods game vs. valuation), so no scoop, but relevant to how Study 1 should interpret whatever persistence it finds.

## Evidence on the eight open questions

**Q2 (does capability move behavior toward the normative benchmark, monotonically?).** arXiv:2609.19843 (dual-process nudge study) independently corroborates the non-monotonicity SSRN 5998954 already established: reasoning effort *redirects* rather than uniformly reduces bias susceptibility — down for default/automatic nudges, up for reflective/social-influence nudges, structured by model scale.

**Q4 (do framing effects transfer?).** arXiv:2609.18357 (Market Signal Injection) adds new evidence: presentation-only manipulations (numerical formatting, competitor ordering, sentiment commentary — never the underlying numbers) measurably shift LLM pricing-agent decisions across 9 open-weight and 3 proprietary models, with larger models no more robust.

**Q5 (what causes LLM hyper-rationality?).** Same dual-process study (2609.19843) bears here too — the redirection-not-reduction pattern is a mechanism-level data point any account (corpus composition, RLHF, low-temperature denoising, procedure-vs-context partition) needs to accommodate. arXiv:2609.18591 offers a complementary angle: apparent in-context "learning" in multi-agent settings looks more like statistical pattern extrapolation than genuine reasoning once historical patterns are disrupted.

**Q7 (do LLM agent markets avoid or produce bubbles/mispricing?).** arXiv:2609.18357 shows sentiment-injection attacks propagate beyond the targeted agent to *other firms* in a simulated market and alter profit/consumer-surplus outcomes — evidence that LLM-agent markets can be distorted by non-fundamental information, not just adversarially poisoned fundamentals.

**Q1 (do personas bind LLM behavior, or are they defeasible?).** arXiv:2609.17989 adds a data point against a purely defeasible account: platform-vs-consumer principal framing persists in reduced skepticism toward sponsored content even after a competing corrective instruction (stricter sponsorship labeling) is applied, though the label does have some independent effect.

No new evidence found this week on Q3 (affect-mediated bias transfer), Q6 (direction of the disposition effect), or Q8 (cross-model/cross-draft reproducibility) beyond the general validity-gap caution in arXiv:2609.20543 (below), which is tangential rather than direct evidence.

Replays 100 human Wason-task deliberation groups with matched belief-anchored LLM-agent groups; LLM groups show 34–44 percentage-point higher full-consensus rates than the matched humans, and simulated consensus doesn't track collective accuracy. A possible instrumentation approach for isolating "belief" from "verbalized anchor" — worth knowing about, not finance-specific.
- **arXiv:2609.15319, "Clean Scores, Buried Evidence, and Confident Wrong: A Receipt-Based Audit of Frontier Agentic QA"** (Luis M. Sánchez; submitted 2026-09-14; preprint, fetched directly). - **arXiv:2609.17632, "EvolveTrade: Experience-Driven Policy Refinement for Self-Evolving LLM Trading Agents"** (Sehee Kim, Yumin Choi, Minki Kang, Sung Ju Hwang; submitted 2026-09-15; preprint).
